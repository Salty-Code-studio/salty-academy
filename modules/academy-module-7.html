01 · The point

The job of this episode

Episode 1 is the promise of the whole channel. One sentence:

The promise

Big companies always had their own software. Now a 44-scooter rental on Bonaire has it too.

You are not selling in this video. You are marking territory. A rental owner should finish it thinking three things:

  • "That's my problem." They saw their own Tuesday on screen.
  • "He's solved it before." They saw it working in a real shop.
  • "I could talk to him." Calm, honest, no hype.

One problem only. Who has which scooter, and who paid? Everything else is a different episode. If you catch yourself drifting into the website or Google, stop. That's another episode.

02 · The one person

Who is watching

Talk to one person. Not "guys." Not "everyone."

The owner

Runs 20 to 80 scooters, cars or boats. Island or tourist town. Works the counter himself. Knows every vehicle by number. Runs the business from his head, a spreadsheet and WhatsApp. It works. Mostly. He's tired.

He doesn't care about code. He cares about his evenings, his cash and his name.

His Tuesday

These are the moments you name. Insiders know them. Outsiders don't. Naming them is the proof.

  • A customer messages at 11 at night. He answers from bed.
  • He opens the spreadsheet. Red means unpaid. Green means paid. Orange means in the shop. He coloured every cell by hand.
  • A student rents for five months. Pays month by month. Was March paid? He scrolls back to check.
  • Someone walks in. "Do you have two for Friday?" He has to check before he can say yes.
  • He's sick for a day. Nobody else can read his system.

Sjonnie approved the episode. On the day, ask him which of these he actually lived. Use his words, not ours.

03 · The craft

Pitching the pain

People don't buy software. They buy the end of a feeling. Your job in the first two minutes is to make that feeling clear. Not bigger. Clearer.

Rule 1. Name the moment, not the category.

"Admin problems" means nothing. "Scrolling a spreadsheet at 11 pm to see if March was paid" hits. The specific moment does the work.

Rule 2. Cost it three ways.

CostWhat it looks likeSay it like
TimeHours a week checking, colouring, answering"That's a part-time job nobody's paying you for."
MoneyRentals that ran on unpaid. Nobody noticed."The money isn't lost. It's just not in your pocket."
ReputationSlow replies. A mix-up at the counter."Tourists don't remember the scooter. They remember the wait."

Rule 3. Do the maths out loud.

The bottleneck bill: hours a week × € per hour × 52. Ask Sjonnie his real hours. If it's 5 hours at €25, that's €6,500 a year. Say the number. Let it sit. Don't explain it twice.

Rule 4. Never mock the old way.

The spreadsheet got him this far. Respect it. "This worked. Until it didn't." The viewer uses the same spreadsheet. Laugh at it and you laugh at him.

Rule 5. Normalise it.

"Every rental we've met runs like this." That's true, and it takes away the shame. Shame closes people. Normal opens them.

Before

  • A rental overview spreadsheet. Cells coloured by hand: red unpaid, green paid, orange in the shop.
  • Bookings by WhatsApp and the old website.
  • Long rentals (students, months at a time) tracked by memory and scrolling.

After

  • The board. Every scooter, every day. Unpaid rentals show red and striped. "NOT PAID" on hover. You see it without looking for it.
  • Paid until. One date per rental. A student pays another month? Tap "+1 month". Done.
  • The phone gate. A booking comes in. Sjonnie gets it on Telegram. Confirm or decline. The customer gets a branded email.
  • Pay at the desk. No online payments forced on him. Island customers pay at the counter, so the system works that way.
  • His history. We imported his old sheets back to February 2025. Nothing was thrown away.

The line you can say

Sjonnie linked his phone and confirmed real bookings from Telegram the same day. That's the whole test. Does the owner actually use it? He did.

Numbers: ask first

When we imported his sheets, we found rentals marked unpaid that added up to real money. That's a powerful moment. Ask Sjonnie before you say the amount. If he says no, say "thousands" or "more than he expected." The moment still lands.

Also confirm with Sjonnie: did double-bookings ever happen? Only say it if yes.

06 · Being the one

How trust gets built on camera

Authority isn't claimed. It's noticed. Six habits make people notice.

  • Insider words. "Plate number." "Turnaround." "Late return with the next renter waiting." Only someone who's been there talks like this.
  • Show, then say. Screen first. Claim second. Or no claim at all.
  • Admit a mistake. True one: when we first read Sjonnie's sheets, we thought they were empty from December. They weren't. The format had changed. We caught it and fixed it. Saying that makes everything else believable.
  • Say what you didn't build. "No online payments. His customers pay at the desk. So we built it that way." Restraint reads as expertise.
  • Slow down. Nervous people rush. Experts pause. Finish the sentence. Breathe. Next.
  • Specific beats impressive. "44 scooters" beats "a large fleet." "Same day" beats "fast."

The test

Would Sjonnie nod if he watched it? If yes, every other owner will too.

09 · Getting watched

Packaging for YouTube

Title options

  • The software that runs a scooter rental
  • How a 44-scooter rental knows who paid
  • We replaced a rental's spreadsheet. Here's the system.

Pick the one you'd search for. People find YouTube by searching.

Thumbnail

Two halves. Left: red and green spreadsheet. Right: the clean board. Three words at most: "Who paid?" Your face optional, but real.

The first 30 seconds

The title made a promise. Repeat it in the first sentence. If the title says "who paid," the first line is about who paid. Anything else and they leave.

10 · Practice

Teach-back drills

Answer out loud. No notes. If you stumble, reread that section and try again tomorrow.

  1. Say the promise of this episode in one sentence.
  2. Name three moments from a rental owner's Tuesday. Make them specific.
  3. Cost the problem in time, money and reputation. One line each.
  4. Do the bottleneck bill out loud with 5 hours a week at €25.
  5. Walk through the fix in three steps: board, paid until, phone.
  6. Monischa asks "What if they pay cash?" Answer in under 15 seconds.
  7. Tell the mistake story. Situation, what went wrong, how we caught it.
  8. Explain why big chains always had this and a shop now can too. Under 30 seconds.
  9. Give the one ask. Then stop talking.
  10. Say "paid until" three ways: pro, everyday, kitchen table.
